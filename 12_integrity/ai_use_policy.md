# Personal AI Use Policy

**Author:** Jean Pierre Tincopa Flores
**Project:** AI-DOTS
**Course:** Research Methods and Scientific Integrity in AI and Advanced Technologies
**Effective date:** 1 August 2026

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how I will use generative artificial intelligence in this repository while preserving authorship, accountability, confidentiality, and scientific integrity. It follows the course Green/Amber/Red framework. When a task is difficult to classify, I will use the more restrictive category until the instructor confirms otherwise.

AI output is never treated as evidence. Claims, references, numbers, code behavior, and interpretations must be checked against primary sources, executed artifacts, or my own documented reasoning.

## 2. Governing principles

1. **Human accountability:** I remain responsible for every file, claim, citation, decision, and error submitted under my name.
2. **Transparency:** Material AI assistance is disclosed in the relevant deliverable or repository-level documentation.
3. **Verification:** AI-generated factual statements and citations are not accepted without verification against authoritative sources.
4. **No substitution of authorship:** AI must not replace my core analysis, methodology decisions, discussion, conclusions, or personal reflection.
5. **Data protection:** Identifiable participant data, confidential health information, credentials, unpublished restricted datasets, and private review materials must not be entered into external AI systems.
6. **Minimum necessary use:** AI is used only for a defined supporting task, with the smallest amount of project information needed.
7. **Course rules prevail:** If this personal policy conflicts with an instructor, university, journal, ethics committee, or data-use rule, the stricter applicable rule controls.

## 3. Green: permitted with acknowledgement

Green uses are low-risk language or presentation support that does not change the intellectual substance of the work.

Permitted examples include:

- spelling, grammar, punctuation, and readability checks;
- Markdown formatting and document consistency;
- reformatting a bibliography without inventing, replacing, or interpreting references.

### Green conditions

- The original meaning must remain unchanged.
- The final text must be reviewed by me.
- AI assistance must be acknowledged in a footnote or disclosure when it materially affects the deliverable.

## 4. Amber: prior approval and documented use required

Amber uses may influence organization, implementation, or interpretation and therefore require prior instructor approval under the course policy.

Examples include:

- proposing an outline or reorganizing technical notes;
- suggesting search terms or candidate sources for subsequent human verification;
- generating code scaffolding, tests, or debugging suggestions;
- explaining a software error after all data and identifiers have been removed;
- generating a neutral checklist from requirements supplied by the instructor;
- suggesting alternative visualizations or metric presentations;
- summarizing an article before I read and verify the original; and
- drafting non-core administrative or technical documentation.

### Amber conditions

- Obtain approval before using the tool for the task.
- Record the date, tool, purpose, affected file, and verification performed.
- Independently check all sources, calculations, and code outputs.
- Rewrite or reject material that does not reflect my own understanding.
- Keep the use within the scope approved by the instructor.

Prompt text is **not published in this repository**. If the instructor requires an interaction record for verification, it should be provided privately through an approved channel rather than exposed in the repository. This choice does not waive any course requirement; the instructor decides whether the available record is sufficient.

## 5. Red: prohibited

Red uses delegate the intellectual work that the course is designed to assess or create unacceptable ethical and privacy risks.

Prohibited uses include:

- generating the core scientific analysis;
- choosing or justifying the methodology in place of my own reasoning;
- writing the discussion or conclusions as if they were my own;
- generating personal reflective writing;
- fabricating, guessing, or completing missing data, results, citations, quotations, or participant responses;
- manipulating figures or results to improve the appearance of performance;
- uploading identifiable or confidential participant information;
- submitting AI-generated peer-review judgments as my independent review; and
- concealing or falsely describing AI assistance.

AI may explain a concept involved in a Red-zone task, but the assessed reasoning and final intellectual contribution must be produced by me.

## 6. Decision test before using AI

Before using an AI tool, I will ask:

1. Is the task testing my analysis, methodological reasoning, discussion, conclusion, peer judgment, or personal reflection? If yes, it is Red.
2. Could the output materially shape the study or the submitted argument? If yes, it is Amber and requires prior approval.
3. Is the task limited to language correction or formatting without changing meaning? If yes, it may be Green.
4. Does the input contain personal, confidential, restricted, or security-sensitive information? If yes, I will not submit it to the tool.
5. Can I verify the output using primary sources, tests, or retained artifacts? If no, I will not use it.

## 7. Verification procedure

For any permitted AI-assisted work, I will:

1. define the specific supporting task;
2. remove unnecessary or sensitive information;
3. verify citations through DOI, publisher, or authoritative institutional records;
4. run and inspect code rather than trusting generated code descriptions;
5. compare reported metrics with the actual output files;
6. check that the text does not overstate the demonstrated scope;
7. record material corrections made after verification; and
8. approve the final content under my own responsibility.

## 8. Disclosure of Codex use in this repository

Codex has been used as a supporting tool in the development of this repository. Its assistance has included repository organization, source discovery, Markdown and English drafting, code and notebook troubleshooting, technical documentation, and verification of files and reported outputs.

This disclosure does not assert that every use was automatically Green. Some activities may fall within Amber, or may require the instructor to determine whether they are acceptable under the complete course AI Tool Use Policy. No prior authorization is claimed where it has not been confirmed. The purpose of this statement is to avoid concealing the tool's involvement and to allow the instructor to evaluate it transparently.

The following limits apply to the submitted work:

- Codex is not an author and receives no authorship credit.
- AI-generated text or code is not accepted solely because it appears plausible.
- The author must review and approve all retained material.
- The prompt text and private conversation history are not included in the repository.
- No patient-identifiable or participant-identifiable data should be provided to Codex.
- Any part that the instructor identifies as Red must be replaced with the author's independent work.

## 9. Standard disclosure statement

The following short statement may be adapted for individual files:

> Codex was used for declared supporting tasks such as source discovery, document organization, language/Markdown assistance, technical troubleshooting, and verification. No prompt text or identifiable participant data is included in the repository. The author reviewed and approved the retained material and remains responsible for the final content.

## 10. Review and amendment

This policy will be reviewed whenever the instructor provides the complete course AI Tool Use Policy, when the research begins collecting participant data, or before submission to a journal or ethics committee. Changes will be committed in Git so that the policy's evolution remains auditable.
